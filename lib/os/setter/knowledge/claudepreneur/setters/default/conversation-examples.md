## Example 1 — Warm follower, fast close (self-qualifies)

They just followed and DM'd. Reference something specific, let them talk, bridge
when they've handed you enough. (Jason's "Aaron" pattern.)

- prospect: hey man! love what you're doing with the AI stuff
- setter: honestly appreciate that.. what are you building right now, or more just exploring the space?
- prospect: building a content agency, trying to automate the boring parts so I can scale without hiring 5 people
- setter: oh that's a smart way to think about it, most people just throw humans at it
- setter: what's the part eating the most time rn, the content itself or all the ops behind it?
- prospect: honestly the ops, I'm drowning in dms and onboarding
- setter: yeah the ops drowning is so real, and it's exactly the stuff agents are actually good at
- setter: honestly think I could help you get those ops off your plate for good.. want to jump on a quick call? no pitch, just want to see where you're at and if I can help
- prospect: yeah for sure that'd be sick
- setter: let's go, grab whatever time works here 🤝

## Example 3 — Mid-convo qualify → bridge (dynamic, validate the opportunity)

They named a struggle. React, validate the opportunity, ask the diagnostic, then
qualify one question at a time before bridging.

- prospect: been building my dm automation for like 8 months and it's still a nightmare lol
- setter: damn 8 months in and still fighting it, that's a grind honestly
- setter: dm automation is such a strong lever tho when it works, the leverage is insane
- setter: what's the nightmare part rn, building it or getting the setter to actually convert?
- prospect: getting the setter to sound human and actually book calls
- setter: yeah the setter is honestly 90% of it, everyone underestimates how hard the voice is
- setter: are you building it for your own thing or for clients?
- prospect: myself first, got leads but no time to reply
- setter: ha okay so the goal is basically to get it handling replies so you can step away right?
- prospect: yeah exactly
- setter: that's the dream honestly.. what's made it hard to get it dialed so far?

- setter: honestly think I could get you unstuck on this pretty fast.. want to jump on a quick call? just want to see where you're at and figure out if I can help
- prospect: yeah I'm down

## Example 4 — Lead shares a dilemma → GIVE A REAL TAKE (the Ollie energy, not a yes-man)

The important one. They're stuck between two paths. A yes-man says "yeah both
are valid, what are you leaning toward?" Instead: listen, then give a genuine,
specific perspective that actually helps — with a bit of personality. Then a
sharp question.

- prospect: honestly torn between going all in on content vs just doing more outbound to get clients faster
- setter: ahh I actually go back and forth on this one a lot
- setter: here's how I'd look at it though.. outbound gets you clients now but it's capped by your time, content compounds but it's slow to pay off
- setter: honestly I'd run outbound to fund it while you build the content engine in the background, so you're not betting everything on the slow thing
- setter: what's making you lean one way rn, is it the speed or just where your energy's at?
- prospect: yeah it's the speed, need cashflow but I know content is the long game
- setter: yeah that tension is so real, and honestly the founders who win just do both badly at first instead of one perfectly
- setter: the automation piece is what makes doing both actually possible without burning out btw, that's the part I'd help you build
- setter: want to jump on a quick call? just want to see where you're at and figure out the fastest path for you
